# Ad Script Review

Validate an ad script the way three people would: a first-time viewer, a harsh critic, and the client's lawyer. Report honestly. A script that is not ready must not be scored as ready.

## Step 1: Establish context

From the script and conversation, determine: product, audience, funnel stage, platform, length and the one action wanted. If two or more are unknown and cannot be inferred, ask in one short message. Otherwise state assumptions at the top.

Count the spoken words. At about 2.5 words per second, compare against the intended length and report any overrun.

## Step 2: Score the fourteen checks

Score each 1 to 5 with one line of evidence quoted or paraphrased from the script.

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Stop power | Would the first second stop a stranger? |
| Right viewer | Does the hook call in the buyer, not just anyone? |
| Promise kept | Does the next line confirm the hook, and does the ending beat the expectation set? |
| Curiosity | Is there an open question pulling the viewer forward at every point? |
| Retention | Does each beat cause the next, with a re-hook every 5 to 8 seconds (30 to 40 in long-form)? |
| Belief | Is there real, specific proof? |
| Hope | Can the viewer picture their own better outcome? |
| Objection | Is the biggest doubt raised and answered before the CTA? |
| Offer clarity | Is the deal clear, the risk removed, the reason to act now true? |
| Action | Exactly one CTA that says what to do and what they get? |
| Shareability | One built-in reason to send it to someone? |
| Voice | Passes the read-aloud test, within the word budget, free of machine-sounding patterns? |
| Native | Would it pass as content the viewer chose to watch on that platform? |
| Honesty | Is every claim supportable and every gap flagged? |

Total out of 70 and convert to a score out of 10. Never round up to 10 while any check is below 5 or any proof is unconfirmed.

## Step 3: First-time viewer pass

Read it as someone who has never heard of the brand and is half paying attention. Note:
- The exact second attention would drop, and why
- Any word or reference they would not understand
- Whether they could say what is being sold after one viewing
- Whether they know what to do next

## Step 4: Critic pass

Read it as a sceptical creative director. Note:
- Lines a competitor could use unchanged
- Adjectives standing in for facts
- Lists of features where a cause-and-effect story should be
- Claims without evidence next to them
- Anything that sounds like an advert rather than a person

## Step 5: Risk pass

Flag, without softening:
- Statistics, testimonials, clients or results that are not confirmed as real
- Urgency or scarcity that may not be true
- Discounts without a real reference price
- Guaranteed outcomes for health, income or money
- Real people or famous names used without clear permission
- Comparative claims about named competitors

## Step 6: Report

Deliver in this order:

1. **Verdict**: score out of 10 and one sentence on whether it is ready to run
2. **Scorecard**: the fourteen checks with evidence
3. **Top five fixes**, most valuable first, each with the exact line to change and a rewritten version
4. **Risk items** the client must confirm
5. **Offer to rewrite**, unless the user already asked for the rewrite, in which case use the master-ad-script-writer skill and re-score the result

## Rules

- Lead with the verdict, not with praise.
- Quote the script when criticising it.
- Do not invent facts to improve a line; mark the gap as `[NEED: ...]`.
- If asked to validate a script this session just wrote, score it as strictly as anyone else's.
