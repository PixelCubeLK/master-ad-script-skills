# Offer and Call to Action

Read this in Step 8, or when a script holds attention but does not convert.

## The value equation

Perceived value rises with the size of the dream outcome and how likely it feels, and falls with the time and effort it takes.

To raise value in a script:
- Make the outcome vivid and specific
- Raise certainty with proof and guarantees
- Shorten the time to first result
- Lower the effort of the first step

## Anatomy of an offer

| Part | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Core promise | What do I get, in one line? |
| What is included | What exactly arrives? |
| Price and anchor | What does it cost, compared with what? |
| Bonuses | What makes saying yes now better? |
| Guarantee | What if it does not work? |
| Scarcity or deadline | Why now? (only if true) |
| Next step | What do I do? |

A script rarely fits all seven. For 15 seconds: promise and next step. For 30: add price or guarantee. For 60: add proof of the guarantee and the reason now.

## Discount and price framing

- State the normal price first, then the deal. The normal price must be real.
- Give the reason for the discount: launch, season, anniversary, limited places. A discount with a reason is believed.
- Percent for small prices, amount of money for large ones.
- Break a large price into a small daily or monthly figure, only if the maths is exact.
- Compare with the cost of the alternative or of doing nothing.
- Bundle rather than cut price where margin matters.
- One offer per ad.

## Risk reversal

Pick the strongest the client can truly honour:
- Money-back guarantee with a stated period
- Free trial, no card
- Pay on delivery
- Cancel anytime
- Free first session, audit or sample
- Results-based guarantee (never for health, income or financial outcomes)

Say the guarantee in plain words, right before the CTA.

## Honest urgency

| True | False, never write |
|---|---|
| Offer ends on a real date | Countdown that resets |
| Limited stock or places that are counted | "Only 3 left" when unlimited |
| Seasonal relevance | Invented demand |
| Price is rising on a set date | "Today only" every day |
| The cost of each week of delay | Threats |

If nothing is truly urgent, use the cost of waiting.

## CTA by awareness and warmth

| Audience | CTA type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, unaware | Soft, curious | "See how it works" |
| Cold, problem-aware | Low-commitment value | "Get the free guide" |
| Warm, solution-aware | Specific next step | "Book your free audit" |
| Warm, product-aware | Offer with risk removed | "Try it free for 14 days" |
| Hot, most-aware | Direct, with deadline | "Order today, offer ends Sunday" |

## Writing the CTA

Formula: action verb + what they get + qualifier.

- Say what they get, not what they must do.
- Tell them where and what they will find there.
- One action only.
- Repeat it on screen and in voice.
- Weak: Submit, Learn more, Click here. Strong: "Get my free plan", "See pricing for my team".
- For UGC, keep it soft and personal: "Try it yourself."

## CTA placement

- Short ads: last 10 to 15 percent.
- 60 seconds and up: a light mention mid-way, full CTA at the end.
- Content-style ads: embed the CTA inside the point it solves.
- Before the CTA, give the viewer the high note: what they gained and what it lets them do.

## Two-step CTAs

Offer a direct step for the ready and a softer step for the unsure: "Start your trial" and "Watch the demo". In video, voice the main one and show the second on screen only.

## Retargeting set

For viewers who watched and did not act:
1. Objection ad: voice the top doubt and answer it.
2. Proof ad: testimonials or results, carousel or montage.
3. Different-offer ad: another product or a smaller first step.

## Closing checklist

- Is the offer one sentence?
- Is the anchor true?
- Is the risk removed?
- Is the reason to act now real?
- Is there exactly one action?
- Does the landing page headline match the ad's promise?
