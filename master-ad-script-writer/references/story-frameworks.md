# Story Frameworks

Read this in Step 4 when choosing the format, and in Step 5 when mapping it onto the master beats.

## Choosing

| Situation | Framework |
|---|---|
| Product replaces a worse old way | Villain and Hero |
| Pain is strong and familiar | Problem, Agitate, Solution |
| Audience is sceptical, proof is strong | Problem, Solution, Proof |
| Product demonstrates well | Hook, Demo, CTA |
| Social proof drives the purchase | UGC Testimonial |
| Result is visible | Before and After |
| Trust and meaning matter most | Founder Story |
| Market believes something false | Myth Breaker |
| Brand message needs clarifying | Hero and Guide (StoryBrand) |
| Very short, direct response | 20-second DR |
| Long-form, educational | Contrarian Lesson |

## Beat maps

Percentages are of total runtime.

### Villain and Hero
1. Hook (10%): curiosity or a direct hit at the old way
2. Villain (25%): what is wrong with the old product, habit or industry; true specifics
3. Hero (20%): the product removes exactly those faults
4. Proof (25%): on-screen test, side-by-side, numbers
5. Objection benefit (10%): answer the top doubt (often price)
6. CTA (10%)

### Problem, Agitate, Solution
1. Problem (15%): name it precisely
2. Agitate (25%): show what it costs them, emotionally and practically
3. Solution (45%): the product, how it works, the result
4. CTA (15%)

Agitation is the step writers skip. Do not skip it; do not overdo it.

### Problem, Solution, Proof
1. Problem (25 to 30%)
2. Solution (40 to 50%)
3. Proof (20 to 30%): testimonial, result, before/after, then CTA

### Hook, Demo, CTA
1. Hook (20 to 30%): a claim worth proving
2. Demo (50 to 60%): prove the claim on screen
3. CTA (10 to 20%)

### UGC Testimonial
1. Personal hook (15 to 25%): "I tried everything for [problem] until..."
2. Experience (50 to 60%): the frustration in detail, discovery, first use, a specific result
3. Recommendation and soft CTA (15 to 25%)

Voiceover only, no stage directions in the spoken text. Two or three benefits at most.

### Before and After
1. After, shown first (10%)
2. Before: the starting state (20%)
3. The turning point: what changed (25%)
4. The process, simply (25%)
5. After, in full, and CTA (20%)

### Founder Story
1. The moment the problem became personal
2. What was tried and failed
3. The insight
4. What was built and for whom
5. Proof it works for others
6. Invitation

### Myth Breaker
1. State the common belief
2. Break it with a fact or demonstration
3. Explain the real cause
4. Show the product as the answer built on the real cause
5. Proof, CTA

### Hero and Guide (StoryBrand)
A character wants something, meets a problem, finds a guide with a plan who calls them to action, avoiding failure and reaching success.

For a script, compress to: who they are and what they want; the villain and the three levels of problem; the brand's empathy and authority; a three-step plan; what is at stake; what success looks like; who they become.

### 20-second DR
| Seconds | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Hook |
| 3 to 7 | Problem |
| 7 to 12 | Solution |
| 12 to 15 | Why it is different |
| 15 to 18 | Social proof |
| 18 to 20 | CTA |

About 50 to 60 spoken words.

### Contrarian Lesson (long-form, 60 seconds and up)
1. Context: what this is about
2. Common belief
3. Contrarian turn
4. Proof of authority
5. Plan: what is coming
6. Points: second strongest first, strongest second, each as what / how / why
7. High-note recap, then CTA as the next step

## Making any framework hold attention

- Each beat must cause the next: join with "but" and "so".
- Open a loop in beat one; close it near the end.
- Put a re-hook at every beat change.
- The viewer is the hero. The product is what helps them win.
- End on capability and relief, never on fear.

## Message that sticks

A script is remembered when it is simple (one core idea), unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, and told as a story. Check the draft against those six.

## Awareness changes the framework's weight

| Stage | Spend most time on |
|---|---|
| Unaware | Story and identity; reveal the problem gently |
| Problem-aware | Agitation and the real cause |
| Solution-aware | Why this approach beats the alternatives |
| Product-aware | Proof and objection handling |
| Most-aware | The offer |
