# Worked Examples

Two complete outputs with invented brands. Use them as a pattern for format and depth, not as copy to reuse. All proof points are marked as needed from the client.

---

## Example 1: General public, 30 seconds, Meta Reels

**Brief:** "Lumo", a rechargeable desk lamp that mimics daylight, for people working from home. Offer: launch price with a 30-day return. Cold audience, problem-aware.

**One-line test:** For home workers who feel drained by mid-afternoon, this shows that their lighting is the cause, so they try Lumo risk-free.

**Format:** Villain and Hero. Villain: dim, yellow room light.

**Hook options**
1. "It's 3pm and you're exhausted. It's your lamp." (pain + curiosity) ✓ pick
2. "Why working from home makes you sleepy." (explainer)
3. "I changed one thing on my desk and stopped needing a nap." (story)
4. "Your desk light is lying to your brain." (shocking statement)
5. "If you work from a spare room, watch this." (call-out)

**Director's script**

| Time | Beat | Visual | On-screen text | Voiceover | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Hook | Person slumps at desk, yellow dim light | 3PM. EXHAUSTED? | "It's 3pm and you're exhausted. It's your lamp." | Dull hum |
| 0:03-0:07 | Pain | Coffee number three, squinting at screen | Coffee #3 | "You've had three coffees and you're still foggy." | |
| 0:07-0:12 | Twist | Split: window daylight vs room bulb | Your brain reads this as evening | "But normal bulbs are so dim your brain thinks it's evening." | Soft click |
| 0:12-0:17 | Reveal | Hand switches on Lumo; desk brightens | LUMO: daylight on your desk | "So Lumo gives your desk the light of a bright morning." | Music lifts |
| 0:17-0:23 | Proof | Light-meter reading side by side; customer clip | [NEED: measured brightness] | "[NEED: customer quote or test result]" | |
| 0:23-0:26 | Vision | Same person, 3pm, alert, finishing work | Done by 5 | "Picture finishing your day with energy left." | |
| 0:26-0:30 | Offer + CTA | Product, price, button | Launch price · 30-day returns | "Try Lumo for 30 days. Send it back if you don't feel it." | Sting |

**Clean voiceover (71 words, placeholders excluded)**
It's 3pm and you're exhausted. It's your lamp. You've had three coffees and you're still foggy. But normal bulbs are so dim your brain thinks it's evening. So Lumo gives your desk the light of a bright morning. [proof line]. Picture finishing your day with energy left. Try Lumo for 30 days. Send it back if you don't feel it.

**CTA variants**
- Soft: "See how Lumo works."
- Medium: "Try Lumo for 30 days."
- Strong: "Launch price ends [true date]. Get yours today."

**Open items:** measured brightness comparison; one real customer quote; true launch end date. No claims about health or sleep without evidence.

## Example 2: Business owners, 30 seconds, LinkedIn and Meta

**Brief:** "Tally", a bookkeeping service for small restaurants. Offer: free review of last month's books. Cold audience, problem-aware.

**One-line test:** For restaurant owners who do the books at midnight, this shows that the hours they lose cost more than the service, so they book a free review.

**Format:** Problem, Agitate, Solution, with an identity call-out.

**Hook options**
1. "Restaurant owners: who does your books at midnight? You." (call-out + pain) ✓ pick
2. "You didn't open a restaurant to do spreadsheets." (relatability)
3. "The most expensive employee in your restaurant is you, doing accounts." (contrarian)
4. "What does one Sunday of bookkeeping really cost you?" (question)
5. "I closed the kitchen at 11 and opened the laptop at 12." (story)

**Director's script**

| Time | Beat | Visual | On-screen text | Voiceover | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Hook | Empty restaurant, owner alone with laptop | RESTAURANT OWNERS | "Restaurant owners: who does your books at midnight? You." | Clock tick |
| 0:03-0:08 | Pain | Receipts in a box, tired eyes | Receipts. Invoices. Payroll. | "After a fourteen-hour day, you're matching receipts." | |
| 0:08-0:13 | Twist | Calendar: Sundays crossed out | Your day off, gone | "And it costs you the one day you could rest or plan." | |
| 0:13-0:18 | Reveal | Phone photo of receipt; dashboard updates | Photo in. Books done. | "So Tally does it for you. Send a photo, and it's handled." | Music in |
| 0:18-0:23 | Proof | Named client, real figure | [NEED: client name and hours saved] | "[NEED: client result]" | |
| 0:23-0:26 | Vision | Owner locks up, lights off, smiling | Close up. Go home. | "Close the kitchen and go home." | |
| 0:26-0:30 | Offer + CTA | Offer card | Free review of last month's books | "Book a free review of last month's books. No contract." | Sting |

**Clean voiceover (64 words, placeholders excluded)**
Restaurant owners: who does your books at midnight? You. After a fourteen-hour day, you're matching receipts. And it costs you the one day you could rest or plan. So Tally does it for you. Send a photo, and it's handled. [client result]. Close the kitchen and go home. Book a free review of last month's books. No contract.

**Why it is built this way**
- Identity word in the first second so only owners stop.
- Emotion (tiredness, the lost day) opens; the logic (hours and cost) sits in the proof.
- Low-risk first step, which suits a business buyer.

**Audience variants:** swap "restaurant owners" and the details for cafés, salons, clinics. Keep the structure.

**Open items:** one named client with a real hours-saved figure; confirmation that "no contract" is accurate.
