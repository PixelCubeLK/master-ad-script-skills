# Audience Psychology

Read this in Step 3, and whenever a script is clear but not moving anyone.

## The five awareness stages

How much the viewer already knows decides what the ad must say.

| Stage | They know | Ad's job | Hook style | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Nothing; no felt problem | Make them recognise themselves | Story, identity, curiosity | Longer |
| Problem-aware | The pain, not the fix | Show you understand, reveal the cause | Pain, question, explainer | Medium to long |
| Solution-aware | Fixes exist, not yours | Show why your way is better | Contrarian, comparison, mechanism | Medium |
| Product-aware | Your product, not convinced | Prove it and remove doubt | Proof, testimonial, risk reversal | Short to medium |
| Most-aware | Wants it, waiting for a reason | Give the offer | Offer, urgency, CTA first | Short |

Quick diagnosis: cold traffic and broad audiences sit in the first two stages; engaged followers and site visitors in the middle; cart abandoners and past buyers at the end.

## Business owners and the general public

| | Business owners | General public |
|---|---|---|
| Wants | Growth, profit, time back, control, standing among peers | To feel better, look better, save money or time, belong |
| Fears | Wasting money, falling behind, being fooled again | Regret, embarrassment, missing out, being scammed |
| Trusts | Numbers, case results, named clients, process, guarantees | People like them, demonstrations, reviews, easy returns |
| Decides | Emotion starts it, logic must justify it | Emotion first, one good reason second |
| Tone | Peer to peer, direct | Friendly, human, specific |
| First step | Low risk: audit, demo, trial | A clear deal with a true reason to act now |

Business owners are also people: a script that only argues return on investment and never touches pride, stress or relief will underperform.

## Emotions that move people to act

- **Relief**: the weight comes off. Strongest after well-drawn pain.
- **Hope**: a believable picture of things going right for someone like them.
- **Belonging**: people like me use this.
- **Pride and status**: this says something good about me.
- **Fear of loss**: what continuing as now will cost. Use lightly.
- **Joy and delight**: pleasure in the product itself.
- **Nostalgia**: a return to something good.
- **Instant gratification**: results soon, effort low.

Vary them through the script. One emotion held for thirty seconds goes flat.

## Principles of influence, used honestly

| Principle | In a script | Honest-use rule |
|---|---|---|
| Social proof | Real numbers, real reviews, real faces | Never invent or inflate |
| Authority | Credentials, tests, expert voices | Only real, relevant credentials |
| Reciprocity | Give a useful tip or free resource first | The gift must stand alone as useful |
| Scarcity | Limited stock, places or time | Only when the limit is real |
| Commitment | Ask for a small first step | Do not trap; make leaving easy |
| Liking | A presenter the audience relates to | Cast truthfully |
| Unity | "We" language for a shared identity | Only where the identity is shared |
| Loss aversion | Show what waiting costs | State real costs, not imagined disasters |
| Anchoring | Show the usual price or the alternative's cost first | The anchor must be a true price |
| Contrast | Old way beside new way | Compare fairly |
| Framing | "Costs less than a coffee a day" | The maths must hold |
| Peak-end rule | Make one high point and a strong ending | Viewers remember those two moments |
| Zeigarnik effect | Open loops keep attention | Always close them |
| Mere exposure | Repeat the brand cue and core line | Consistency across variants |
| Paradox of choice | One offer, one CTA | Do not present five options |
| Curse of knowledge | Explain as if they have never heard of it | Test on an outsider |

## The psychology of giving hope

1. Show you see their situation exactly.
2. Name the real cause, so the problem feels solvable rather than personal failure.
3. Show someone like them who got through.
4. Make the first step small.
5. Remove the risk of trying.

False hope sells once and costs the client reviews, refunds and ad accounts.

## Objections

Every viewer holds one main doubt. Find it before writing: ad comments, reviews, sales calls, forums.

| Common doubt | Answer in the script |
|---|---|
| Too expensive | Cost over time, cost of the alternative, guarantee |
| Won't work for me | A case that matches them closely |
| Tried something like it | Why this differs in mechanism |
| No time | How little effort the first step takes |
| Don't trust the brand | Proof, named clients, visible process |
| Can do it later | True cost of waiting, true deadline |

Voice the doubt in the script before answering it. Viewers trust an ad that says what they were thinking.

## What kills conversion

- Hook unrelated to the product
- Problem stated but not felt
- Generic solution any competitor could claim
- Proof that is vague or unbelievable
- Urgency that is obviously fake
- A CTA asking for more commitment than the ad has earned
