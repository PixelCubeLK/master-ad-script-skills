# Hook Library

Read this when writing the five hook options in Step 6, or when a hook is not landing.

## How to pick a hook

1. Fix the viewer's awareness stage (see `audience-psychology.md`).
2. Pick the emotional trigger that fits the stage.
3. Pick a tactic that carries that trigger.
4. Write it in three layers: seen, said, written on screen.
5. Check relevance: would a non-buyer stop for this too? If yes, narrow it.

## The eight triggers

| Trigger | What it does | Best at stage |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Opens a question the viewer needs closed | Unaware, problem-aware |
| Pain agitation | Names a frustration in their own words | Problem-aware |
| Pattern interrupt | Breaks what they expect to see or hear | Any, strongest on crowded feeds |
| Identity call-out | Names who this is for | Any; essential for niche offers |
| Contrarian / myth-busting | Contradicts a common belief | Solution-aware, sceptical audiences |
| Social proof / credibility | Shows others or experts already trust it | Product-aware, hesitant |
| Urgency / stakes | Shows the cost of waiting | Most-aware, offer periods |
| Aspiration / desire | Shows who they could become | Unaware, lifestyle and status products |

## Tactic library

Each tactic is a frame for the first line. Fill the brackets with the client's real details.

**Audience and identity**
- Demographic call-out: "For [specific group] who [situation]."
- If-then: "If you [condition], do this."
- Direct address: "If you're seeing this, [reason it concerns them]."
- Relatability: "When [everyday moment they know too well]..."
- Aspirational: speak to who they want to become.
- Exclusivity: "Only for [serious group]." Use only when access really is limited.

**Curiosity and surprise**
- Curiosity: a statement that cannot be true at first glance, but is.
- Shocking statement: "You've been [doing common thing] wrong."
- Contrarian: an expert who does the opposite of what experts advise.
- Myth-busting: "[Common belief] is false. Here's why."
- Reverse psychology: "Don't watch this if [condition]." Use sparingly.
- Explainer: "Why your [thing] keeps [problem]."
- Warning: "Don't buy another [category] until you see this."

**Proof and credibility**
- Authority: credential, certification or expert backing. Must be real.
- Social proof: number of customers, reviews or sales. Must be real.
- Statistic: a sourced number that reframes the problem.
- Confession: "I was wrong about [topic]."
- Reasons why: "3 reasons [experts/customers] choose this."

**Story**
- Storytelling: drop in mid-moment. "Three years ago I was [situation]."
- First time / second time: what they got wrong first, what they do now.
- Result first: show the outcome, then rewind.
- Challenge: "I bet you can't [task]."

**Structure**
- How-to: "How to [result] in [time]."
- Listicle: "5 ways to [result]."
- Question: mirror the question already in their head.
- Contrast: put two things side by side to expose a mismatch.
- Directive: "Stop [habit]. Start [better habit]."

**Offer and action** (warm and hot audiences only)
- Offer only: the deal itself is the hook.
- Price anchor: "Less than [familiar small cost] a day."
- Risk reversal: lead with the guarantee.
- Urgency: a true deadline or limit.
- CTA first: "Shop the [offer] now."
- FOMO: others are already switching. Use only if true.

## Native spoken openers

Conversational patterns that sound like a person, not a brand. Adapt, never paste.

- A secret being shared at last: "I've kept quiet about this, but..."
- Late discovery: "How did nobody tell me that [fact]?"
- Honest confusion: "Here's what I don't get about [category]."
- Hot take with a twist: "My unpopular opinion is [X], and here's what complicates it."
- Lesson learned: "The first time I [did X] I [mistake]. Now I [better way]."
- Time-boxed plan: "If I had 90 days to [goal], here's what I'd do."
- Hyper-specific question: "This is only for [narrow group]: are you [doing the thing]?"
- Casual heads-up: "Quick heads up about [thing they use]."
- Say one thing, show another: spoken line and visual deliberately disagree, then resolve.

## Visual hooks

The picture must make the same point as the words.

- Motion in the first frame: a hand, a pour, a cut, an unboxing.
- A slightly risky or odd action that begs to be resolved.
- The product in an unexpected state (covered, broken open, oversized).
- A visual metaphor for the pain (only if the link is instant).
- A face reacting before the viewer knows to what.
- Split screen: old way versus new way.
- Text on screen from the first frame, readable in under two seconds.

## Platform shaping

| Platform | Hook shape |
|---|---|
| Meta feed | First line of copy plus first frame; works muted; under 125 characters before the fold |
| Reels / TikTok / Shorts | First 1 to 1.5 seconds; spoken and on-screen text together; native, unpolished |
| YouTube skippable | Hook and promise before the 5-second skip |
| YouTube bumper (6s) | The hook is the whole ad |
| LinkedIn | Role or industry call-out; a number; no hype |
| Radio / audio | Sound or a question in the first second; say the brand early |

## Hook quality checks

- Specific: a number, a name, a moment. Not an adjective.
- Honest: the ad can deliver what the hook promises.
- Targeted: the right person knows it is for them.
- Short: one breath, under 12 words spoken.
- Not an ad opener: no greeting, no logo, no "Introducing".

Write hooks across at least three different triggers so the test set is not five versions of one idea.
