# Voice, Delivery and Production

Read this when writing the voiceover, preparing director's notes, or shaping a script for a platform.

## Writing for the ear

- One idea per sentence. Most sentences under 15 words.
- Contractions. Everyday words.
- Numbers written as they are said.
- No tongue-twisters; avoid runs of "s" and "p" sounds.
- Put the important word at the end of the sentence.
- Mark pauses with a line break or an ellipsis.
- Read it aloud against a timer before delivering.

## Word budgets

About 2.5 words per second for calm delivery, up to 3 for energetic.

| Length | Words |
|---|---|
| 6s | 12 to 15 |
| 15s | 30 to 40 |
| 20s | 50 to 60 |
| 30s | 65 to 80 |
| 45s | 100 to 120 |
| 60s | 130 to 160 |
| 90s | 200 to 230 |

Leave air. A full budget leaves no room for music, reaction or a product shot.

## Sounding human

Avoid:
- "It's not X, it's Y" reveals
- Lists of negatives ("no X, no Y, no Z")
- Self-answered questions ("The result?")
- Three-part lists by reflex
- Stock openers ("In today's world", "Whether you're")
- Empty praise words: seamless, powerful, revolutionary, game-changing, unlock
- Every sentence the same length

Do:
- Use the customer's exact words
- Mix short and long sentences
- Be specific where a competitor would be vague
- Let one line be plain for every line that is punchy

## UGC voice

- A friend recommending something, not a presenter.
- Specific personal detail beats general praise.
- Imperfect on purpose: a restart, an aside, a laugh.
- Leave gaps for the creator's own words.
- Soft CTA.

## Voiceover brief (for the director's notes)

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Voice | Age range, warmth, pace, accent |
| Emotion arc | Where it starts, turns and ends |
| Emphasis | The three words that must land |
| Pace | Words per second; where to slow down |
| Reference | "Like talking to one person across a table" |
| Pronunciations | Brand and product names |

Direction that works: describe who the speaker is talking to and why, not "more energy". For AI voices, mark emotion cues in brackets and keep sentences short.

## Shot and edit notes

- New picture every 2 to 3 seconds in short-form.
- Movement in frame one.
- Captions always; most social video is watched muted.
- Keep text out of the top and bottom interface zones in vertical formats.
- Show the product in use by the midpoint.
- Proof on screen while it is spoken.
- End card: offer, CTA, brand.

## Platform shapes

| Platform | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meta Reels and Stories | 9:16 | Native feel, captions, hook in the first second |
| Meta feed | 1:1 or 4:5 | First line of copy works as a second hook |
| TikTok, Shorts | 9:16 | Unpolished, trend-aware, text plus motion at once |
| YouTube skippable | 16:9 | Promise before 5 seconds; can run long if it earns it |
| YouTube bumper | 16:9, 6s | One message |
| LinkedIn | 1:1 or 16:9 | Professional, subtitles, demo or case study |
| TV | 16:9 | Slower, brand early and late, legal lines |
| Radio, audio | none | Paint the picture in words; brand named two or three times; CTA repeated |

## Static companions

For each video script, offer a static set:
- Three headlines (different triggers)
- Two body copy lengths: short (under 125 characters) and long (story form)
- One line of on-image text
- One CTA button label

## Variation plan

One concept ships with:
- 3 to 5 hooks on the same body
- 2 CTA strengths
- 2 to 3 audience versions (identity word and details changed)
- Vertical and one other ratio

## Testing order

Test the element with the biggest effect first: hook, then offer and CTA, then body format, then visuals. Change one thing per test. Give the client the winning headline to mirror on the landing page.
